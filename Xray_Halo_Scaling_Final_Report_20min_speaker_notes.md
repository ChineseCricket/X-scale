# X-ray Halo Scaling Final Presentation: speaker notes

## Slide 1: Project title
Introduce the scientific aim: use Chandra X-ray measurements to test how hot-gas observables trace weak-lensing calibrated halo mass in CLASH + LoCuSS clusters. State the headline result: the mass-based scaling relations are stable to core excision within current uncertainties.

## Slide 2: Contents
Use the contents slide as a formal roadmap: background physics, sample selection, Chandra reduction, spectral modeling, scaling inference, and results.

## Slide 3: Why galaxy clusters matter
Frame clusters as massive dark-matter halos and laboratories for intracluster-medium physics. The key point is that cluster observables connect cosmology and baryonic physics.

## Slide 4: From halo mass to X-ray luminosity
Explain the causal chain: halo mass sets the potential depth, the potential sets gas temperature and density, and thermal bremsstrahlung produces X-ray luminosity.

## Slide 5: Why real clusters are more complex
Use this slide to motivate intrinsic scatter. Cooling, AGN feedback, mergers, gas clumping, and projection change the relation away from the ideal self-similar expectation.

## Slide 6: What this project does
Connect the background to the project workflow: select lensing-calibrated clusters, reduce Chandra data, model spectra, fit scaling relations, and interpret how well X-ray gas traces halo mass.

## Slide 7: Sample definition
Explain the selection funnel from parent sample to 18-cluster main sample. Emphasize that Abell 0209 is a retained example, while Abell 0697 is a processed object excluded by spectral QA rather than one of the seven visual-drop clusters.

## Slide 8: Chandra reduction
Clarify the separation between imaging products and spectral products. Merged images are used for morphology, peak finding, and masks; spectral fitting keeps per-ObsID ARF/RMF responses.

## Slide 9: Aperture construction
Use the Abell 0068 overlay to show how the smoothed X-ray map, X-ray peak, point-source masks, and R500-scale aperture geometry enter the measurement.

## Slide 10: Spectral fitting model
Present the model components: blank-sky particle background, XRB sky-background components, and the absorbed APEC ICM source model. Note that the fit band is 0.7-7.0 keV and WSTAT is used.

## Slide 11: Spectral QA
Explain that the QA plot separates raw source/background behavior from the net source and folded source-region model. This avoids misreading raw background-containing source counts as a direct model mismatch.

## Slide 12: ACCEPT sanity check
Introduce ACCEPT as an external temperature reference used for plausibility checks. It is not a prior or calibration input; large discrepancies flag cases needing QA review.

## Slide 13: Scaling inference
Define the log-linear fixed-evolution fit and pivots. The main presentation focuses on L_X-M500 and T_X-M500 because they directly test X-ray observables against weak-lensing halo mass.

## Slide 14: Full-R500 mass-based results
Give the headline full-R500 results: L_X-M500 beta = 1.08 with 0.169 dex scatter, and T_X-M500 beta = 0.48 with 0.117 dex scatter for the 18-cluster main sample.

## Slide 15: Core-excised comparison
Explain the core-excised 0.15-1.0 R500 test. The corresponding slopes, 1.16 for L_X-M500 and 0.56 for T_X-M500, remain consistent with the full-R500 results within current uncertainties.

## Slide 16: Quality control
Summarize why five systems are excluded from the headline fit and how leave-one-out tests support the stability of the mass-based slopes. Keep the emphasis on controlled interpretation rather than overclaiming precision.

## Slide 17: Scientific conclusion
Close the main talk here. The project demonstrates a complete Chandra-to-scaling-relation workflow; hot-gas observables broadly trace weak-lensing halo mass, but precision is limited by sample size, background modeling, heterogeneous coverage, and high-temperature systems.
